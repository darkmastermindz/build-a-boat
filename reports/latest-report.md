<details><summary><code>drag.force</code>: F = ½ · ρ · v² · Cd · A   (ρ = 1.94 slug/ft³, v in ft/s, 1 mph = 1.467 ft/s)</summary>

1. Cd·A = 0.9 × 0.43 (blunt drum ends) + 0.5 × 2.23 (pointed bow) = 1.50 ft²
2. 1 mph: ½ × 1.94 × 1.47² × 1.50 = 3.1 lb; power = F × v = 3.1 × 1.47 × 1.356 = 6 W

## Whitewater simulation (default settings)

### How each number was worked out

#### ww.channel

**Manning (SI): Q = (1/n) · A · R^(2/3) · √S, A = b·h, R = b·h ÷ (b + 2h);  U = Q ÷ A;  Fr = U ÷ √(g h)**

1. Competition channel (the race course): Q = 15 m³/s, slope S = 0.021 (6.4 m drop over 300 m)
2. Assumed width b = 12 m and roughness n = 0.045
3. Normal depth h = 0.588 m (1.93 ft), solved by bisection
4. U = 15 ÷ (12 × 0.588) = 2.12 m/s (4.8 mph)
5. Fr = 2.12 ÷ √(9.81 × 0.588) = 0.88 (subcritical: standing waves can form)

The channel average: how fast and deep the water runs between features. The boat drifts at about this speed. Width and roughness are not published, so they are assumptions you can change.

Sources: [Wikipedia: Manning formula (V = (1/n) R^(2/3) S^(1/2), SI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manning_formula) · [Wikipedia: Hydraulic roughness (boulders raise n)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_roughness) · [Wikipedia: Froude number (Fr = v ÷ √(g h))](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Froude_number) · [Wikipedia: U.S. National Whitewater Center (channel lengths, drops, slopes, flows, named rapids)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Whitewater_Center) · [Designer: S2O Design: U.S. National Whitewater Center project](https://s2odesign.com/projects/the-u-s-national-whitewater-center/) · [Wikipedia: Kraków-Kolna canoe slalom course (Olympic-standard channel 12 m wide)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w-Kolna_Canoe_Slalom_Course) · [Wikipedia: Canoe slalom (course current at least 2 m/s)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoe_slalom)

#### ww.period

**T = 2π · r ÷ √(g · GM)  (r = radius of gyration about the centre of gravity)**

1. Pitch: r = √(Σ w·(Δx² + Δy²) ÷ W) = 19.9", GM = 46.4" → T = 0.93 s
2. Roll: r = √(Σ w·(Δz² + Δy²) ÷ W) = 11.8", GM = 3.2" → T = 2.13 s
3. g = 386.1 in/s². The water dragged along with the drums (added mass) is left out, so the real periods are somewhat longer.

Every floating body rocks at its own natural period, like a pendulum. Waves met at that period pump the motion up (resonance).

#### ww.waves:upper-waves

**Standing wave: U² = (g/k)·tanh(k h), λ = 2π/k;  breaking at H ≤ 0.142·λ·tanh(k h);  boat balanced on η = (H/2)·cos(k x − φ)**

1. Class II wave height (top of the class range): 3 ft = 0.91 m
2. Current U = 2.12 m/s, depth h = 0.588 m → k = 1.597 1/m, standing wavelength 3.93 m (12.9 ft)
3. Steepest a 0.91 m wave can be: H/λ = 0.142 → λ ≥ 6.44 m. Used λ = 6.44 m (21.1 ft): at the breaking limit, so expect a breaking, foaming crest; the boat is 2.36 m long
4. Steepest surface slope atan(π H ÷ λ) = 24.0°
5. Balanced at 16 positions on head waves (crests across the boat) and beam waves (crests along it):
6. Largest trim 23.2°, largest heel 35.0°, lowest frame clearance -8.3", lowest deck clearance -4.1" (after the breaking crest)
7. Every position balances
8. Meeting the waves: T_e = λ ÷ U = 3.03 s vs natural pitch 0.93 s and roll 2.13 s; undamped magnification 1 ÷ |1 − (T_n/T_e)²| = 1.10 (pitch), 1.98 (roll)

The waves stand still while the boat drifts through them at the current speed. A Class III wave at the top of its range is taller than the channel-average flow can hold, so it is treated as the steepest wave of that height that can exist. ### Limits

- Quasi-static: the boat is balanced on the water surface at each position. Fast flow, splash, impacts and paddling are left out.
- Wave and drop sizes come from the class definitions and estimates, not measurements at the venue.
- Use this to compare designs and spot weak points, not as a guarantee. Wear the venue's PFDs and helmets.
